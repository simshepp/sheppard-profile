Simon Sheppard is a Master's student in the Cognitive Science department. He received his BA in Honours Psychology from the University of British Columbia in 2024. His current research interests include basic income, poverty and cognition, as well as sustainability.

Working Paper

A Guaranteed Basic Income in Canada: Framework Design and Public Support

With rising poverty and economic insecurity in Canada, a call has emerged for a national guaranteed basic income (GBI). In response, we designed a framework for a GBI delivered to economic families equal to 70% of the poverty line, adjusted based on family size and geographic region, with a stable reduction rate of $0.50 for every $1 of net income earned to minimize disincentives for work. To finance the GBI, we developed three funding models that offset the cost of the GBI through changes to the current tax and transfer system, resulting in a net cost model ($23.7B), a net zero model ($0B), and a net savings model (-$29.6B), each reducing poverty by 57.0% to 64.5%. To assess public support for GBI, we conducted three pre-registered national surveys where Canadians voted for any or none of the GBI models. Across all surveys more than 80% of Canadians voted for at least one GBI model, with the most votes for the net cost model (39.9%), followed by the net zero model (32.5%) and the net savings model (15.9%). This work elucidates policy pathways and support for a basic income program in Canada to reduce poverty and economic insecurity.
